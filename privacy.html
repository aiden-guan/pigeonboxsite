Privacy

You choose where it runs.

The extension, your AI provider, your server and your tracker have separate data flows.

On this page

On this computerYour serverTrackingAnalyticsDelete and back up

On this computer

Your browser stores the local mailbox index, settings and drafts. On-device models and local Ollama inference keep AI processing on your machine. A remote endpoint receives the context needed for each request when you configure one.

Gmail, model downloads, tracking and opt-in update checks need network access. Your API keys stay in trusted extension contexts and are not handed to Gmail’s page.

Your PigeonBox server

If you deploy a server and connect Google, your Workers process mail through the Gmail and Calendar APIs. Your Supabase project stores metadata and derived data such as summaries, drafts and search information. Sensitive stored content and Google credentials are encrypted with your server’s keyring.

Your configured AI provider receives the context required by enabled features. Review its retention settings and the permissions you grant. The repository’s privacy policy and data-flow documentation describe these boundaries in detail.

Tracking is optional

Your tracker receives subjects, sender and recipient addresses, message identifiers and activity events. It does not receive message bodies. Tracked PDFs are uploaded to your server’s private storage bucket for the document viewer.

Image proxies, blocked images, scanners and sender views affect activity signals. An open does not establish that someone read the mail.

No PigeonBox usage telemetry

This website does not submit analytics events, collect waitlist addresses or connect visitors to a PigeonBox account service. The extension sends no usage analytics. The website host and the services you configure may keep their own operational access logs.

Delete and back up

Clear local data in Settings or uninstall the extension. Use your server’s privacy controls for stored account data; deleting local extension data does not delete your server’s records.

You control database backups, retention and server encryption keys. Keep your keys private and backed up. Read the security boundaries.
